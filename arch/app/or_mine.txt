 LP CONVERSION — DO FIRST, IN SEQUENCE (this is the actual build)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1) Multi-period Storage LP Formulation (SOC balance; why the charge flag drops)
  PROMPT: "I'm an ML/data scientist converting a rule-based battery dispatch sim
  into an exact LP. Teach me the canonical multi-period storage LP: decision vars
  charge[t], discharge[t], SOC[t] over 8760 hours; the SOC balance constraint
  SOC[t] = SOC[t-1] + eta_c*charge[t] - discharge[t]/eta_d; bounds min_SOC/max_SOC,
  power caps Pmax, initial/terminal SOC. Then prove the key result I'm relying on:
  with round-trip efficiency eta_c*eta_d < 1, a cost-minimising LP never charges
  and discharges in the same hour, so the usual binary charge/discharge flag is
  redundant and I can stay in pure LP. Show exactly when this breaks (negative
  prices, paid curtailment, rewards for cycling). Give the full LP written out and
  3 interview questions with answers."

2) Linearization Techniques (min/max, clamps, if-else priority, piecewise cost)
  PROMPT: "Teach me how to linearise logic that's currently written as Python
  rules so it becomes LP constraints. My dispatch sim uses min()/max(), SOC
  clamps, an if-else 'discharge first else charge from solar then wind then
  market' priority, and merit-order stacking. For each pattern show the LP trick:
  replacing min/max with inequalities + the objective's own pressure, expressing
  priority/merit order via cost ordering instead of if-else, piecewise-linear
  convex costs, and which rules CANNOT be linearised without a binary (so I know
  what I'm approximating). Give before/after examples mapping a rule to
  constraints, and 3 interview questions."

3) Economic Dispatch & Unit Commitment as LP (the formulation my model IS)
  PROMPT: "Give me the power-systems framing of my problem. Explain economic
  dispatch and unit commitment: the hourly demand-balance constraint
  (sum of generation + discharge - charge + market = demand), merit-order /
  marginal-cost dispatch, generation limits, and why dispatch is an LP but unit
  commitment (on/off startup) is a MILP. My model is zonal with a shared battery
  and continuous sizes — map my sizing+dispatch problem onto this standard form,
  and explain what I lose by dropping the commitment binaries. One worked small
  dispatch LP and 3 interview questions."

4) Simplex vs Interior-Point (Barrier) + Crossover for large sparse LPs
  PROMPT: "I have an LP with ~8760 time steps and thousands of constraints.
  Explain, at a practitioner level (no KKT derivations), how to solve it fast:
  primal/dual simplex vs the barrier/interior-point method, why barrier usually
  wins on large sparse LPs, what crossover does and when to turn it off, and how
  warm-starting helps when I re-solve per session. Tell me which Gurobi method
  setting to pick and how to read the solve log. 3 interview questions."

5) Time-coupled / Block (Staircase) LP Structure & Sparsity
  PROMPT: "Explain the structure of a multi-period LP where SOC[t] couples hour t
  to t+1. Show why this gives a block/staircase constraint matrix, why it's huge
  but very sparse, and why that sparsity is what makes 8760-hour LPs tractable.
  Cover how to exploit it (sparse formulation, avoiding dense big-M), and mention
  when decomposition by time would ever help. Tie it to keeping my model fast
  enough to run per user session. 3 interview questions."

6) LP Degeneracy & Multiple Optima (why LP and heuristic can both be 'right')
  PROMPT: "Teach me LP degeneracy and multiple optimal solutions, with energy
  dispatch as the example. Explain why dispatch LPs are highly degenerate (many
  equal-cost dispatches), what that means for solution uniqueness, and critically
  why it makes shadow prices / duals non-unique. Then connect it to my pipeline:
  when my exact LP and my fast heuristic report the same cost but different builds
  or different marginal prices, how much of that 'deviation' is real sub-optimality
  vs just alternate optima? How do I tell them apart? 3 interview questions."
