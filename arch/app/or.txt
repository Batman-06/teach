===============================================================================
            OPERATIONS RESEARCH — INTERVIEW PREP PRIORITIZATION
===============================================================================
A tiered guide to what actually gets tested in serious OR interviews,
organized by return-on-investment of study time.


===============================================================================
 TIER 1 — NON-NEGOTIABLE CORE
 (Tested in almost every serious OR interview)
===============================================================================
The backbone of whiteboarding, algorithmic questioning, and design rounds.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Phase 1: Foundations
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Polyhedral Theory & Basic Feasible Solutions (BFS)
    Crucial. Clearly explain the equivalence between extreme points, vertices,
    and basic feasible solutions (Ax = b, x >= 0).

• Strong Duality & Complementary Slackness
    Guaranteed interview questions. Expect dual interpretations, economic
    equilibrium, and optimality verification via slackness.

• Sensitivity Analysis & Shadow Prices
    Fundamental for business-translation rounds
    (e.g., "What does a non-zero dual variable mean to the VP of Supply Chain?").

• Decomposition (Benders & Dantzig-Wolfe)
    Critical for senior roles. Know when to use:
      - Benders        -> complicating variables / block structure
      - Dantzig-Wolfe  -> complicating constraints

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Phase 2: Integer & Combinatorial
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Branch and Cut
    The engine of commercial solvers. Know branching strategies, node
    selection, and LP relaxation bounds.

• Column Generation & Branch-and-Price
    Standard for crew scheduling, cutting stock, and vehicle routing.
    Expect: how the pricing problem generates columns with negative
    reduced cost.

• Lagrangian Relaxation
    Standard for finding tight bounds and explaining subgradient steps.

• Total Unimodularity (TUM)
    Classic screening question: why TUM matrices guarantee integer
    LP solutions.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Phase 3: Network Optimization
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Max-Flow / Min-Cut Theorem
    Asked constantly — both mathematically and as a modeling trick.

• Minimum Cost Flow & Network Simplex
    Fastest algorithms in linear network design; basic formulation and
    basis-tree mechanics are expected.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Phase 7: Solvers & Production Reality
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Gurobi / CPLEX Internals
    Presolve mechanisms, IIS (Irreducible Infeasible Subsystems) for
    debugging infeasible models, lazy constraints vs. user cuts, and
    MIP gap definitions.

• Numerical Stability
    Coefficient ranges (10^6 vs 10^-3), big-M choices, and condition numbers.


===============================================================================
 TIER 2 — SITUATIONAL
 (High value depending on the role / domain)
===============================================================================
Prepare these for companies in logistics, supply chain, cloud capacity,
or pricing.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Phase 4: Stochastic & Robust OR
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Two-Stage Stochastic Programming & L-Shaped Method
    Vital for energy-grid sizing, capacity planning, and supply chain
    network design under demand uncertainty.

• MDPs (Value / Policy Iteration)
    Essential if the team touches reinforcement learning, dynamic pricing,
    or sequential decision-making. Rarely tested in pure math-programming
    roles.

• Robust Optimization
    Know the intuition (worst-case polyhedral / budgeted uncertainty sets).
    Exact derivation of ellipsoidal duals is rarely asked on a whiteboard.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Phase 5: Queueing & Inventory
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Inventory Theory (Newsvendor, EOQ, (s, S))
    A must for retail, e-commerce, or manufacturing (Amazon, Walmart,
    Target). Less critical for scheduling / energy roles.

• Queueing Theory & Little's Law
    Standard for telecom, cloud scheduling (AWS/GCP), and ride-hailing
    (Uber/Lyft).

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Phase 6: Metaheuristics
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• ALNS & Large Neighborhood Search
    Non-negotiable if the company solves last-mile delivery, vehicle
    routing (VRP), or large-scale bin packing where exact MILP fails at
    scale. For pure scheduling / blending, basic simulated annealing or
    local-search intuition suffices.


===============================================================================
 TIER 3 — IGNORABLE / LOW ROI
 (Skip for initial prep)
===============================================================================
Weeks of study time, near-zero yield in 95% of industry interviews.

• Phase 1 — Revised Simplex Implementation
    Know how the tableau works conceptually and why sparse LU factorization
    is used. Memorizing step-by-step matrix inversion by hand is wasted time.

• Phase 1 — Interior Point Methods (Primal-Dual Path-Following)
    Basic intuition is plenty (barrier parameter mu -> 0, Newton step toward
    the central path). You'll virtually never derive the KKT-system steps on
    a whiteboard.

• Phase 1 — Farkas' Lemma
    Duality and complementary slackness are tested directly; formal Farkas'
    Lemma proofs are academic filter questions.

• Phase 2 — Matroid Theory
    Beautiful math, but interviewers stick to TUM or greedy bounds on graphs.

• Phase 2 — Detailed Cut Lifting Mechanics
    Knowing definitions of Gomory cuts and knapsack covers is enough; manual
    coefficient lifting is overkill.

• Phase 3 — Cycle-Canceling
    Network simplex and basic min-cost flow formulations suffice;
    cycle-canceling proofs are academic.

• Phase 4 — Chance-Constrained Optimization
    Hard to solve in non-convex form without sample approximations. Rarely
    probed beyond: "how do you linearize a VaR / chance constraint using
    binary variables?"

• Phase 5 — Jackson Networks & Complex Queueing Proofs
    Little's Law and basic M/M/1 or M/M/s formulas are enough.

• Phase 6 — GRASP / VNS Variants
    Stick to the core intuition of ruin-and-recreate (ALNS) and Tabu Search.


===============================================================================
 QUICK REFERENCE — PRIORITY MAP
===============================================================================
  TIER 1 (learn cold)   : BFS/Polyhedra, Strong Duality + Slackness,
                          Sensitivity/Shadow Prices, Benders & Dantzig-Wolfe,
                          Branch & Cut, Column Generation / Branch-and-Price,
                          Lagrangian Relaxation, TUM, Max-Flow/Min-Cut,
                          Min-Cost Flow / Network Simplex, Solver internals,
                          Numerical stability.

  TIER 2 (role-driven)  : 2-Stage Stochastic / L-Shaped, MDPs, Robust Opt,
                          Inventory (Newsvendor/EOQ/(s,S)), Queueing/Little's,
                          ALNS / LNS.

  TIER 3 (skip first)   : Revised Simplex impl, Interior Point derivations,
                          Farkas' Lemma proofs, Matroid Theory, Cut lifting,
                          Cycle-Canceling, Chance Constraints, Jackson
                          Networks, GRASP/VNS.
===============================================================================
